Mathematical Definition
Objective Function

Minimize $y$ defined by:

$$ y = \mathbf{x}^t \mathbf{Q} \mathbf{x} $$

where $\mathbf{x} \in \{0, 1\}^n$ and $\mathbf{Q}$ is a symmetric matrix.

Problem Formulation and Reduction

Solve constrained combinatorial problems in the form:

$$ \begin{align} \min y &= \mathbf{x}^t \mathbf{Q} \mathbf{x} \\ \text{subject to: } \mathbf{A}\mathbf{x} &= \mathbf{b} \end{align} $$
1. Linear to Quadratic: Any linear function in binary $x$ is quadratic ($x_i = x_i^2$).
2. Order Reduction: Higher-dimensional terms can be reduced via penalty variables.
The Penalty Method

Constraints are incorporated into the QUBO objective function by adding quadratic penalties $P$. An inequality like $x_1 + x_2 \leq 1$ is transformed into an unconstrained minimization:

$$ \min y = \mathbf{x}^t \mathbf{Q} \mathbf{x} + P(x_1 x_2)^2 $$
  • Choosing $P \gt 0$ ensures violating the constraint is energetically unfavorable.
  • All penalty terms are squared to ensure the term is never negative.
Penalty Encoding Pairs

Common logical constraints and their corresponding quadratic penalties:

Classical Constraint QUBO Penalty
$x_i + x_j \leq 1$$P(x_i x_j)$
$x_i + x_j \geq 1$$P(1 - x_i - x_j + x_i x_j)$
$x_i + x_j = 1$$P(1 - x_i - x_j + 2x_i x_j)$
$x_i \leq x_j$$P(x_i - x_i x_j)$
$x_i + x_j + x_k \leq 1$$P(x_i x_j + x_i x_k + x_j x_k)$
$x_i = x_j$$P(x_i + x_j - 2x_i x_j)$
An example of Order Reduction

Reduce a cubic term $x_1 x_2 x_3$ to quadratic form by defining $y_1 = x_1 x_2$:

1. Transformation:

$$ y = y_1 x_3 + x_2 x_3 + \dots $$

2. Penalty Term:

$$ P(x_1 x_2 - 2x_1 y_1 - 2x_2 y_1 + 3y_1)^2 $$

3. Recursion:

Higher order terms follow the same logic:

$$ x_1 x_2 x_3 x_4 \rightarrow y_1 x_3 x_4 \rightarrow y_2 x_4 $$

with penalties enforcing $y_1=x_1 x_2$, etc.

General Linear Inequalities and Slack Variables

To encode an inequality $\sum_{i=1}^n a_i x_i \leq b$, introduce a slack variable $s \in [0, b]$ such that $\sum a_i x_i + s = b$. This is implemented by adding a penalty term to the objective:

1. Binary Expansion:

$$ s = \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} 2^i s_i $$

where $N = \lfloor \log_2(b) \rfloor$ and $s_i \in \{0, 1\}$.

2. Penalty Term:

$$ P\left(\sum_{i=1}^n a_i x_i + s - b\right)^2 $$

where $P \gt 0$ is the penalty weight.

The General Form of QUBO
Unconstrained Minimization

The general form including penalty terms $P_i g_i(\mathbf{x})$ is:

$$ \min y = \mathbf{x}^t \mathbf{Q} \mathbf{x} + \sum_i P_i g_i(\mathbf{x}) $$
Conversion to Ising Model

The Ising model uses spin variables $s_i \in \{-1, 1\}$. To convert a QUBO problem to an Ising model, apply the linear transformation:

$$ x_i = \frac{s_i + 1}{2} $$

where $x_i = 1$ corresponds to $s_i = 1$ (spin up) and $x_i = 0$ corresponds to $s_i = -1$ (spin down).

Transformation of the Objective Function

Substitute $x_i = \frac{s_i + 1}{2}$ into $x^t Q x$ to obtain the Ising Hamiltonian $H(s)$:

$$ \begin{align} x_i x_j &= \frac{s_i + 1}{2} \cdot \frac{s_j + 1}{2} \\ &= \frac{1}{4} (s_i s_j + s_i + s_j + 1) \end{align} $$

This maps QUBO to the Ising Hamiltonian form: $H(s) = \sum J_{ij} s_i s_j + \sum h_i s_i + C$.

Parameters:

$$ \begin{align} J_{ij} &= \frac{Q_{ij}}{4} \\ h_i &= \frac{Q_{ii}}{2} + \sum_{j \neq i} \frac{Q_{ij}}{4} \\ C &= \sum \frac{Q_{ii}}{2} + \sum \frac{Q_{ij}}{4} \end{align} $$
Circuits and Hamiltonians

Quantum gates are implemented via time evolution under a Hamiltonian $H$, described by the unitary operator:

$$ U(H, t) = e^{-iHt/\hbar} $$

Implementing a circuit that exactly exponentiates a Hamiltonian with many non-commuting terms is challenging. We use the Trotter-Suzuki decomposition formula to approximate the evolution.

Trotter-Suzuki Decomposition

To implement an approximate time-evolution unitary for $H = \sum_k H_k$:

$$ e^{A+B} \approx \left(e^{A/n} e^{B/n}\right)^n $$

This allows us to approximate the full evolution operator $U(H, t)$ by a product of simpler unitaries:

$$ U(H, t, n) = \prod_{j=1}^n \prod_k e^{-iH_k t / n} $$
QAOA Algorithm
Process Flow
  1. Define cost Hamiltonian $H_C$ and mixer Hamiltonian $H_M$.
  2. Construct circuits $e^{-i\gamma H_C}$ and $e^{-i\alpha H_M}$.
  3. Build circuit: $U(\gamma, \alpha) = \prod_{k=1}^p e^{-i\alpha_k H_M} e^{-i\gamma_k H_C}$.
  4. Optimize parameters $\gamma, \alpha$ classically and measure.
QAOA Intuition
  • An approach to find the ground state of $H_c$.
  • Prepares a quantum state where low-energy states have high probabilities.
  • Probabilistic: It does not guarantee the optimal solution but provides high-probability candidates.
QAOA Circuit Structure

The algorithm alternates between cost unitaries $e^{-i\gamma H_C}$ and mixer unitaries $e^{-i\beta H_M}$:

Repeated $p$ times to increase accuracy.

Hamiltonian Roles
Mechanics
  • $H_C$: Applies a phase depending on the cost $f(x)$. Since $H_C |x\rangle = f(x) |x\rangle$, it maps $e^{-i\gamma f(x)}$ to each state.
  • $H_M$: Mixes the amplitudes to shift probability towards better solutions (lower energy).
QAOA for Knapsack Problem

1. Cost Hamiltonian ($H_c$):

$$ e^{-i\gamma_i (\sum J_{ij} Z_i Z_j + \sum h_i Z_i)} $$
  • $e^{-i\gamma_i J_{ij} Z_i Z_j} \rightarrow RZZ(2\gamma_i J_{ij})$
  • $e^{-i\gamma_i h_i Z_i} \rightarrow RZ(2\gamma_i h_i)$

2. Mixer Hamiltonian ($H_M$):

$$ e^{i\beta_i X}, \quad X = \sum \sigma_i^x $$
  • Implemented via $R_X(-2\beta_i)$ gates.
  • Sequence repeated $p$ times for optimization.
Initialization and Adiabatic QAOA

QAOA can be viewed as a Trotterization of the Quantum Adiabatic Algorithm.

  • Process: Start in ground state $|+\rangle^{\otimes n}$ of mixer $X$ and move slowly to ground state of $H_c$.
  • Adiabatic Schedule:
    • Move $\beta_i$ linearly from $1 \rightarrow 0$.
    • Move $\gamma_i$ linearly from $0 \rightarrow 1$.
