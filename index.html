QUBO Formulation of Combinatorial Optimization Problems

Given a vector $x$ of binary decision variables $$ x = (x_1, x_2, ..., x_n) \in\ \{0, 1\}^n $$ QUBO attempts to minimize the optimization objective $y$ defined as $$ y = x^t Q x $$ where $Q$ is a symmetric matrix of constant coefficients.

Goal: Solve the QUBO problem

$$ \begin{align} \min y &= x^t Q x \\ A x &=b \\ \end{align} $$

where $A$ and $b$ have integer components, representing constraints.

$y$ may be a linear or quadratic function in $x$, but may be assumed to be quadratic without loss of generality (note that $x_i = x_i^2$, for $x_i \in \{0, 1\}$)

$y$ may be higher dimensional, but can be reduced to a quadratic form by introducing penalties.

Constraints

Most optimization problems have constraints, e.g. restricting some $x_i, x_j$ from being simultaneously 1.

These are described by inequalities, e.g. $$ x_i + x_j \leq 1 $$ How can we encode this in our QUBO formulation? Using known inequality/quadratic penality pairs:

Classical Constraint QUBO Penalty
$x_i+x_j\leq 1$ $P(x_i x_j)$
$x_i+x_j\geq 1$ $P(1-x_i-x_j+x_ix_j)$
$x_i+x_j = 1$ $P(1-x_i-x_j+2x_ix_j)$
$x_i\leq x_j$ $P(x_i-x_ix_j)$
$x_i + x_j + x_k \leq 1$ $P(x_ix_j + x_ix_k + x_jx_k)$
$x_i = x_j$ $P(x_i + x_j - 2x_ix_j)$

For example, if our QUBO is described by $$ \begin{align} \min y &= x^t Q x \\ x_1 + x_2 &\leq 1 \\ \end{align} $$ then we encode the constraint as the quadratic penality $P(x_1 x_2)$, where $P>0$ is chosen in a way that ensures the constraint is satisfied, giving us an equivalent unconstrained QUBO formulation: $$ \min y = x^t Q x + P(x_1 x_2) $$ For example, one may choose a large $P$ to avoid both $x_1 = 1$ and $x_2 = 1$ simultaneously.

Slack Variables as general way to encode constraints

Let's say we have a constraint $$ \sum_{i=1}^n a_i x_i \leq b $$ We turn this inequality into an equality by introducing a slack variable $0\leq s\leq b$ represented in binary form $$s = \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} 2^i s_i$$ where $N = \lfloor{\log_2(b)}\rfloor$, and $s_i \in \{0, 1\}$. Then the constraint becomes $$ \sum_{i=1}^n a_i x_i + s = b $$
